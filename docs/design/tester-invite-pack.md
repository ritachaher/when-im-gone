# Tester invite pack — When I'm gone

Everything you need to invite your first 10–20 testers. Copy-paste into email, WhatsApp, or whatever you use.

---

## The ask, in one sentence

You have an early version of an app that helps people gather the practical information their family will need one day. You want 15 minutes of their time to try it, so you can fix what's confusing before other people use it.

---

# Feedback form — 5 questions

Set this up as a free Google Form (forms.google.com) or Typeform. Both are free and take 10 minutes. Older users can fill in a Google Form on their phone easily.

## The form

**Title:** When I'm gone — quick feedback

**Intro text:**
Thanks so much for trying it. These 5 questions should take 2 minutes. Your answers go only to me.

---

**1. Did the setup make sense?** (multiple choice)
- Yes, straightforward
- Mostly — but I hesitated at one point
- I got stuck

**2. If you hesitated or got stuck, where?** (long-answer text, optional)
> Tell me in your own words. Even "I wasn't sure what this button did" is gold.

**3. Did anything scare you or feel uncomfortable?** (long-answer text, optional)
> This is a sensitive topic. If any wording, question, or moment felt off — please say. I'd rather fix it now.

**4. Would you trust this with your real information one day?** (multiple choice)
- Yes, I'd use it for real
- Maybe — I have questions first
- Probably not

**5. What questions do you still have?** (long-answer text, optional)
> Anything. Technical, emotional, practical.

---

## Why these 5 and not more

Each extra question cuts response rate roughly in half for non-technical testers. 5 is the sweet spot:
- Q1 tells you if the funnel is working
- Q2 gives you the specific things to fix
- Q3 is the sensitivity check — for an end-of-life app, this matters more than any other question
- Q4 is your "would you pay?" signal in a friendlier form
- Q5 catches anything the structured questions missed

---

# What to do with the answers

After 5–10 testers have replied:

1. **Fix anything mentioned by two or more people.** That's a real pattern, not a preference.
2. **Ignore single-voice preferences unless you agree.** One person's "I wish the font was bigger" isn't a signal.
3. **Take Q3 (scary/uncomfortable) seriously even if only one person mentions it.** This app's whole thesis is warmth — tone problems matter more than feature bugs.
4. **Track the Q4 "no" answers.** If more than a third of people wouldn't use it for real, something fundamental is off and no amount of feature work will fix it.

Come back and we'll figure out what to build next, together.
